Lead context

Size matters most when it changes the insider's position.

A large dollar purchase can look impressive while adding little to an already-large holding. PlainSight keeps reported position change, role, clusters, and historical sample size beside the headline value.

Methodology

A filing is evidence.
It is not a forecast.

01

Filter the transaction

Corporate signals focus on direct, non-derivative, open-market common-stock purchases. Grants, gifts, exercises, withholding, derivatives, and ambiguous ownership stay out of the ranked list.

02

Score conviction

Transaction size, estimated position change, senior role, repeat or cluster activity, and mature follower history contribute to a transparent research score.

03

Respect publication time

Backtests enter only after information is public. Congressional analysis separately accounts for ranges, ownership labels, and the legal reporting delay.

04

Benchmark the outcome

Measure the same holding window against broad-market benchmarks, include losing signals, and keep low-sample results close to neutral.

Research and risk notes

Use the filing. Keep the skepticism.

PlainSight uses already-public information from official filings. It does not use or facilitate material nonpublic information, execute trades, or connect to a broker.

Filings are delayed, reported ownership can be incomplete, sales may reflect taxes or diversification, political disclosures often use ranges, and past performance does not guarantee future results. This site is educational research—not financial, legal, or investment advice.
